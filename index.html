Plant Biology

Optimization of Low-Cost Organic Media for In Vitro Culture

This research investigated the potential of Gliricidia sepium leaf extracts as a low-cost, locally available supplement for in vitro culture media. The model organism Daucus carota (carrot) was chosen for its well-characterised callus induction pathways.

Key objectives included mitigating tissue browning — a common obstacle in tropical plant tissue culture — and reducing reliance on expensive imported reagents. The study evaluated multiple extract concentrations, observing callus growth rates, colour indices, and cell viability over a 6-week period.

Preliminary results suggest that a 15 % (v/v) G. sepium supplement can reduce browning incidence by up to 40 % relative to control media, opening a promising avenue for resource-limited laboratories across Sri Lanka.

Ecology

Dry Zone Plant Inventory Survey

A systematic floristic survey was conducted across the Rajarata University campus and its immediate surroundings, spanning the dry-zone climatic belt of north-central Sri Lanka. The study employed stratified random quadrat sampling along transects covering grassland, scrubland, and secondary-growth forest habitats.

Species richness, relative abundance, and diversity indices (Shannon–Wiener, Simpson's) were calculated for each habitat type. Particular attention was paid to the successional dynamics of secondary-growth forests recovering from past agricultural use.

The inventory documented over 120 vascular plant species and provides a baseline dataset that can inform future conservation planning and land-use decisions on campus.

Environmental

Environmental & Field Analyses

This body of work encompasses structural field research at two major reservoirs — Deduru Oya and Yan Oya — in the north-western and north-central provinces of Sri Lanka. Water samples were collected following standard protocols for physico-chemical and microbiological analysis.

Parameters measured included pH, dissolved oxygen, biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), total dissolved solids (TDS), and coliform counts. Results were interpreted within the framework of National Environmental (Quality Standards) regulations.

Additionally, a detailed Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) methodology module was completed, covering screening, scoping, impact prediction, and mitigation planning stages — skills directly transferable to consulting and regulatory roles.

Biodiversity

Butterfly Diversity Assessment

A comparative biodiversity assessment was carried out across the Mihintale sanctuary and Rajarata University campus, focusing on butterfly (Lepidoptera) species richness, abundance, and family-level composition.

Transect walks and point counts were employed during morning activity windows to maximise detectability. Surveys covered disturbed habitats (agricultural edges, roadsides) as well as semi-natural scrubland and forest fragments.

Early findings indicate higher Nymphalidae and Pieridae representation in edge habitats near agricultural land, while Lycaenidae and Papilionidae were more prevalent in intact forest patches. The dataset provides a baseline for monitoring pollinator health and habitat quality in the dry zone.

Microbiology

Applied Microbiology

Novel Fermented Beverage: A probiotic-rich beverage was developed using Hibiscus rosa-sinensis and Clitoria ternatea (Butterfly pea) floral extracts fermented with a ginger bug starter culture. The project evaluated pH changes, microbial diversity, and organoleptic properties over a 7-day fermentation period.

Botanical Pesticide Formulation: Following traditional Ayurvedic practice, the Dasha Parna Kashaya (Ten-Leaf Decoction) was prepared and tested for insecticidal efficacy against common agricultural pests. This project bridges ethno-botanical knowledge with modern quality-control assays.

Both projects demonstrate the integration of microbiology, food science, and traditional knowledge systems — a unique interdisciplinary approach that characterises my research ethos.

Ethology

Courtship Behaviours of Peafowls

This ethological study focused on the courtship behaviour of Pavo cristatus (Indian Peafowl) in the dry-zone wildlife corridors surrounding the Mihintale sanctuary and Rajarata University campus.

Observations were conducted during the breeding season using focal-animal sampling and continuous recording methods. Key behavioural parameters documented included courtship display frequency, train-rattling duration, wing-shaking displays, and female approach/avoidance response rates.

Preliminary analysis reveals that display frequency peaks during early morning and late afternoon activity windows, and that males with more elaborate ocelli patterns elicit significantly higher female approach rates. These findings contribute to understanding sexual selection dynamics and behavioural ecology in a semi-urban dry-zone setting.

The study also noted the influence of habitat disturbance on display-site selection, with males preferring semi-open clearings adjacent to dense scrub cover — information valuable for wildlife corridor planning.
